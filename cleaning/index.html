How to price a house cleaning job

Start from cost, not from a competitor's price

Add up what the job really costs you: every cleaner's hours times what you pay per hour (include your own time and employer costs), travel, supplies, and a fair share of overhead like insurance and software. That total is your floor. Any price below it loses money.

Margin is not markup

A 30% margin means 30% of the price is profit. That needs a price of cost divided by 0.70, not cost times 1.30. The calculator does this for you, which is why its number is higher than a simple markup.

Recurring clients

Weekly and biweekly homes usually take fewer hours after the first deep clean. Lower the hours for repeat visits instead of cutting your margin.
